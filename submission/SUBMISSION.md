## Project title
CROMO — every factory runs on reasons nobody remembers

## Short description (one sentence)
CROMO reads a plant's own archive with Google's open Gemma 4 on AMD GPUs (AMD Token Factory) and recovers why every process step exists: it flags costly "fossil" steps whose reason is gone, protects "fence" steps that look pointless but prevent accidents, and warns when a change breaks either.

## Long description
Every factory recipe contains steps nobody can explain anymore. In Primo Levi's "Chromium" (The Periodic Table, 1975),
a paint plant still added ammonium chloride to every batch twenty years after the bad pigment it was meant to fix had
disappeared. That step had become a fossil. Chesterton's fence is the opposite warning: some steps that look pointless
are load-bearing. Plants carry both, and now AI optimisers are starting to propose "remove this, shorten that".

CROMO is an investigator for process engineers, quality/EHS managers and plant managers. Pick any step and it digs
through decades of the plant's own paperwork, hop by hop, streaming each hop to the screen:
- scanned change requests, faxes, deviation and incident reports;
- handwritten Italian shift logbooks;
- supplier qualifications, regulations, emails and old recipe revisions.

It finds the record that introduced the step, follows the references it cites, and checks whether each condition
behind it still holds. It also looks for counter-evidence.

Every verdict is FOSSIL, FENCE, ACTIVE or UNKNOWN, with the reasoning chain, its dependencies and an evidence-strength
grade. Every claim cites a quote verified verbatim in the source. Language models read the evidence; deterministic,
auditable rules decide what may be removed. A fence is never removed, a fossil only through a controlled trial with
rollback, and an unknown goes to a person.

Around the verdicts, CROMO offers:
- **Tripwires:** alerts when a supplier, material, equipment or regulation change makes a step useless, affects a
  fence, or brings an old fossil back to life.
- **"Before you change":** checks proposed changes (blocked / caution / allowed with trial); also a REST endpoint for
  optimisation tools.
- **Cost:** the annual cost of each fossil from explicit MES/ERP inputs.
- **Safe trials:** controlled trial plans approved by an engineer through an Evolus workflow.
- **Veteran interviews:** for steps nobody documented, CROMO asks a retired operator the right questions and files the
  answers as testimony, weighted below written records.

Every model call runs on the AMD Token Factory (shared endpoints on AMD GPUs):
- **Gemma 4 31B-IT (Google DeepMind), open weights** as both the eyes and the brain: it reads every scanned, faxed and handwritten Italian page, reasons over the evidence, proposes verdicts and writes the bilingual search keywords for each document;
- retrieval: BM25 plus local n-gram vectors on the CROMO server, made bilingual by the keywords Gemma writes at ingest (the Token Factory serves no embedding model);
- the vision model was chosen by measurement: on the archive's 68 scanned pages, Gemma-4-31B-IT reads with a 2.2% character error rate (0.7% on handwritten logbooks), against MinerU2.5-Pro 30.2%, DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Vision-Exp 15.0%, MiniCPM-V-4.7-35B-A3B 2.1% for the other AMD-hosted models we tested.
<!-- AMD-STACK:END -->

Evolus building blocks, all driven from CROMO through the Evolus REST API:
- a **workflow** for trial approval (created via `POST /workflows` and run from the Trials page): it writes the engineer's brief with an AI Tool step, records the approval and returns the decision;
- the **compare AI Tool** for "what changed when this step was added" between recipe revisions;
- a **knowledge library** holding the 178-document archive.
<!-- EVOLUS:END -->

Because no real 30-year archive is available, we built a fully synthetic one for a fictional coatings plant:
- 178 documents with realistic scan noise;
- planted ground truth: 12 fossils, 9 fences, 22 active and 6 unknown steps;
- deliberate traps: red herrings, contradictions, a reverted change, a reason found only in a handwritten logbook,
  and a prompt-injection document.

We measure FENCE recall (missing a fence is the dangerous error), the false-FOSSIL rate and citation precision against
it, next to a no-LLM heuristic baseline. The product never reads the answer key (enforced by a test).

<!-- AMD-RESULTS:START -->
**Measured on AMD.** On the planted ground truth CROMO reaches a FENCE recall of 89% (rule-based baseline without an LLM: 56%), a false-FOSSIL rate of 0% (baseline 11%), 0 fences called fossils and a citation precision of 71%. It ingests 1.5 archive pages per minute on the shared event endpoint under heavy load, reads scans and handwriting with a character error rate of 2.2% and takes 21 minutes for a median multi-hop investigation (the shared endpoint answered each model call in about a minute during our run). A full plant audit (178 documents, 49 steps) took 1351 requests and 1.3M tokens, $0.04 on the Token Factory meter (free during the event).
<!-- AMD-RESULTS:END -->

The repository is MIT licensed, keys stay server-side, secrets are scanned in CI, and the app also runs end to end
offline in a clearly labelled fixture mode, so anyone can try it without a GPU.

## Problem and intended user (for the "clear explanation" requirement)
- **Problem:** process recipes accumulate steps whose reasons are forgotten. Some waste money every batch (fossils);
  some silently prevent fires, recalls and claims (fences). Nobody can tell them apart without weeks of archive work,
  and AI optimisers now propose removals without that context.
- **Users:** process engineers (find and trial fossils), quality and EHS managers (protect fences, audit trail),
  plant managers (cost and risk), and optimisation/MES vendors (the change-check API).
- **Measurable gain:** annual cost of fossils found, fences protected from removal, hours of archive digging replaced
  by a cited investigation, and every change gated by a human approval.
